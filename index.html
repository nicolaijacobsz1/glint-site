Stop the photo your DLP can't see.

Glint notices when a phone camera is aimed at a confidential document and blanks that document. The rest of the screen stays as it was, and the attempt is recorded.

For Windows 11 fleets. We'll only email you about Glint.

Every other leak path has a control. The camera doesn't.

Endpoint and browser DLP inspect what people copy, upload, print and email. A phone held up to a screen skips all of it. It takes a second, it needs no permissions, and it leaves nothing in a log.

Policies and posters tell people not to do it. Glint is the control that notices when they do.

How it works

  1. A classified document gets focus.Glint reads the sensitivity label. Only Confidential and above turn the camera on, and it turns off again when the document closes or loses focus.
  2. It watches for a phone in front of the screen.Any phone in view counts, because a video can start pointed at the desk and swing up. Detection runs on the laptop itself, and camera frames don't leave the device.
  3. The document goes black straight away.Just that window, on whichever monitor it is on, blanked by the operating system rather than the app, so a modified client can't ignore it.
  4. The attempt is recorded.Time, device, document label and detection scores go to your security team. An evidence frame is optional and stays in your own storage.

Covering the camera doesn't get around it

If the camera is covered, unplugged or switched off, classified documents stay hidden until it is working again. A working camera becomes a condition for viewing them, not an optional extra.

That is a policy choice, and some sites can't allow cameras at all. For them we are building a mode that blocks screenshots and screen recording of classified documents without using the camera.

Designed to get through a privacy review

  • The camera is on only while a classified document is open, not all day.
  • No video is stored, ever.
  • No face recognition and no biometric templates.
  • The cloud console receives metadata only. Evidence frames stay with you.
  • People see a short notice before opening a classified document, so nobody is monitored without knowing.

What Glint promises, and what it doesn't

Detecting a camera and then blanking the screen is a race against a shutter, and a fast enough photo can still win. We won't sell it as prevention.

What Glint does is make capture deliberate, visible and recorded. People are told before they open the document. A careless snap of the pricing model stops being an easy mistake. A deliberate one leaves evidence.

Before asking anyone to roll it out, we'll publish the false-blank rate measured in our pilot.

Questions

Does Glint record video?

No. Glint never stores video. When an attempt is detected it logs the time, device, document label and detection scores. A single evidence frame can be kept if your policy turns it on, and it stays in your own storage.

Will it blank my screen when I check my phone?

It can. Glint treats any phone in view of a classified document as a possible capture, because a video can start pointed at the desk and then swing up to the screen. Administrators choose how sure Glint must be that it is looking at a phone, so normal work is interrupted only as much as each site needs. We are measuring this in daily use now and will publish the numbers before the pilot.

What hardware does Glint need?

A Windows 11 device with a working webcam, built in or USB. No special camera is required, and Glint shares the camera with Teams calls instead of locking it.

What happens if someone covers the camera?

Classified documents stay hidden until the camera is working again. Covering the lens, unplugging the camera or turning it off doesn't get around Glint.

Does it work without a network connection?

Yes. Detection and blanking run on the device itself, so they keep working when it is offline. Events are sent to your security team when the device reconnects.

Can Glint stop every photo?

No. Detecting a camera and then blanking the screen is a race against a shutter, and a fast enough photo can still win. Glint makes capture deliberate, visible and recorded.
